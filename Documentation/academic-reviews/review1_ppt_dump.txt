Introduction
Small food businesses lose money on both sides of the same problem: over-order and waste perishable stock, or under-order and lose sales with no forecasting tool built for their scale.
THE PROBLEM
Over- and under
-
ordering drives food waste and lost revenue for small operators, with no forecasting tool built for their scale.
WHY IT PERSISTS
AI-driven forecasting has matured for large retail chains, but remains inaccessible to small operators without dedicated IT or data teams.
THE 
TRADITIONAL 
APPROACH
Small operators currently rely on manual spreadsheets, simple moving averages, or pure gut-feel. These static methods completely fail to account for dynamic demand shocks like sudden weather changes or public holidays.

Problem Statement
Small food businesses like cafes, bakeries, independent restaurants make daily purchasing decisions with no reliable way to predict tomorrow's demand.
The core disconnect: 
a cafe owner does not think in RMSE or MAPE — they want to know exactly how many loaves of bread, croissants, or cups of coffee to order tomorrow or make. Across the forecasting literature we reviewed, most studies (Aqila et al., Jatte et al., Gartner et al., Susanto et al.) stop at computing an error metric and never take the final step of producing a purchase quantity.
Forecast-to-Action Gap
Predictions rarely convert into an operational restock quantity.
Accessibility Gap
Accurate tools assume enterprise dashboards and IT staff.
Infrastructure Gap
Best-performing systems assume IoT hardware or heavy compute.
Cold-Start Gap
Deep learning models fail on the short, sparse histories real SMEs.

Motivation and Need for the Project
Small food businesses operate on thin margins — a single bad week of over- or under-ordering has a direct, measurable financial impact.
Perishable inventory means forecasting errors translate directly into food waste and lost revenue, not just a missed KPI.
Existing accessible tools (e.g., “Stock Easy”, Janchidfah et al. 2026) manage inventory manually but have no predictive capability at all.
Enterprise-grade AI/ERP forecasting exists, but survey evidence shows SMEs cannot adopt it — not due to distrust, but due to cost and know-how.
Adoption Barrier
Perceived ease of use is the top factor in whether SME owners adopt AI tools at all (Yuniarty et al., 2025)
0.76
correlation (r) between user satisfaction and digital-transformation readiness among SME retail users (Janchidfah et al., 2026)
30 to 40%
of predicted food-sector sales fail to materialize, due to various unaccounted factors (Jain et al., 2024)

6
Objectives of the Project
01
Build the Forecasting Model
Design an adaptive Prophet + 
XGBoost
 hybrid Prophet for trend/seasonality, 
XGBoost
 for residual correction with regime-specific tuning for sparse and high-volume items.
02
Generate Item-Level Forecasts
Produce daily, item-level demand predictions from the trained hybrid model, validated for consistency across sparse and high-volume item categories.
03
Recommend Restock Quantities
Convert raw forecasts into actionable restock quantities using a configurable safety-stock buffer that accounts for current inventory levels.
04
Explain via LLM Narrator
Integrate an LLM layer that translates the restock output into a plain-language recommendation — understandable without technical or data literacy.

Feasibility of Implementation
Where the Approach Is Strong
•  Facebook Prophet: no GPU, minimal tuning, native seasonality & holiday support — configuration, not custom code
•  LSTM considered, not chosen — needs 1,000+ days of history we don't have
•  XGBoost alone was ruled out weak on sparse SME data (Susanto et al.); as a Prophet residual-corrector it's proven (Kamboj et al., 12–18% MAPE gain)  this is what we use
•  Restock logic is deterministic Pandas/NumPy — engineering work, not a research problem
Where This Creates an Opening
•  No reviewed study combines a Prophet
 
+
 
XGBoost
 hybrid, adaptive density-based model selection, restock-quantity conversion, and a zero-setup mobile interface with plain-language explanation in one system
•  
It
 is positioned to fill that specific combined gap for small food businesses
Where We're Stretched Thin
•  No real business data yet  public datasets (French Bakery, Rossmann) used as proxies
•  
1
1
-week technical build compressed across 3 people running parallel workstreams
•  The weather regressor is the one genuinely custom integration in the pipeline
What Could Work Against Us
•  Weather API rate limits on the free tier could reduce regressor reliability at scale
•  Public-dataset proxies may not fully represent a real cafe/bakery's demand pattern accuracy may not transfer 1:1 to live deployment
