Abstract — Effective and location-intelligent sales forecasting is 
a strategic requirement within the emerging context of smart 
retail. The approach proposed in this study is a hybrid forecasting 
framework based on Facebook Prophet to achieve a robust time-
series pattern decomposition with XGBoost to capture nonlinear 
effects of sales drivers on the residual. This study uses the 
Rossmann drugstore chain dataset in Germany to predict state-by-
day sales with internal factors (promotions, assortments mix, store 
format) as well as external ones (public holidays, proximity of 
competitors). Strong statistical and machine-learning baselines, 
such as naive, seasonal naive, Holt, Winters and SARIMA, and 
stand-alone Prophet and XGBoost, are benchmarked against the 
hybrid. Results indicate that the proposed algorithm can deliver 
consistent improvements across the 16 German states, cutting 
median state-wise MAPE by 12 - 18% compared to the best non-
hybrid baseline. The framework produces explainable and 
accurate forecasts because it leverages the XGBoost complex 
nonlinearities with the interpretability of Prophet. In addition to 
predictive performance, the model has region-specific 
explanations: promotional effect, holiday effect, and seasonal 
variation transpose differently across states, which can be used as 
evidenced-based directions when setting inventory, promotional 
schedules, and area network intensification. The results constitute 
a scalable statistically-oriented predictive framework in the 
context of a competitive retailing scenario, and some of its aspects 
are especially new, such as regarding the use of Prophet-XGBoosts 
hybrid in geo-disaggregated pharmaceutical pharmaceutical retail 
sales. 
Keywords——  Retail Sales Forecasting, XGBoost, 
Facebook Prophet, Time Series Prediction, Geo-Spatial 
Forecasting  
I. INTRODUCTION  
 The retail market has changed drastically over the past years 
following the emergence of e-commerce, technological 
advancement, and globalization. Such radical changes have rendered retail operations to be very complicated and they need 
precise and swift decision frameworks. Of these, accurate sales 
forecasting has come to play a strategic role in achieving market 
decisions related to marketing, finance, inventory and supply 
chain logistics. Precise and accurate forecasting is associated 
with the opportunity of overstocking or stock-out; both of 
which directly impact profitability and customer satisfaction. 
Retail forecasting process is, however, a non-trivial process due 
to the ever-responsive influences of the external factors which 
include government policies, technological changes, trends in 
consumer preferences and competitive pressure. Forecasting 
models have no assurance that they will come up with reliable 
predictions unless the models can adjust to such conditions. 
Well-established statistical predictive models including Auto-
Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model, 
exponential smoothing and the Holt-Winters methodology have 
heretofore offered the bedrock within the predictive modeling 
of time-series [1]-[3]. Simultaneously, by using machine 
learning, predictive models, including regression analysis and 
decision trees have been implemented to extract the past sales 
pattern to extrapolate future demand [4], [5]. More 
sophisticated methods such as clustering-based methods and 
artificial neural networks (ANMs) have also enhances the 
accuracy of forecasting [6]-[8]. The work on retail forecasting 
over the last ten years has also illustrated how forecasts are 
commonly generated at the business-wide and product level, 
information which can be used in making business-level 
strategic and operations decisions about product placements, 
pricing, purchases and inventories [9]-[11]. Regardless of these 
methodological advances, the aspect of retail, although 
dynamic, has made the forecast hard because retail is defined 
by government regulation and innovation and consumer 
preferences and the competition. Consequently, retail 
forecasting has become a very important research topic in order 
to empower companies to successfully predict demand and 
align their strategies to meet it. IEEE ICTMOD 2025 1571168443

II. RELATED WORK 
A. Hybrid Forecasting in Retail and Related Domains 
Hybrid strategies that integrate statistical time-series and 
machine-learning have exhibited steady accuracy 
improvements. An ARIMA ensemble coupled with Prophet and 
a "Prophet + XGBoost" ensemble detected linear trends as well 
as seasonality and residual nonlinearity in a case study in 
Rossmann, with the lowest RMSE of all models tested [12]. A 
ProphetSVR -combo handled holiday and price impacts 
through decomposition and residual modeling and enhanced 
multivariate grocery/retail forecasts [13]. Short-term power 
systems forecasting cross-domain evidence also supports the 
usefulness of hybrids albeit in terms of a reduction of error in 
the mixing of complementary models [14]. Earlier work in retail 
applications continues to focus on aggregate or one store/one 
product forecasts rather than disaggregated chains 
geographically [15], [16]. Hybrid modeling has potential 
although little is being applied to multi-region retail chains.

B. Methodological Overviews 
Wider surveys and technical discussions list modeling 
decisions, data problems, and evaluation approaches that apply 
to retail forecasting [17]. 
C. Traditional Time-series Models and Multivariate Casual 
Perspectives 
Other than hybrids, classical families- 
ARIMA/AR/MA/SARIMA, exponential smoothing, 
HoltWinters- are traditional benchmarks to determine 
seasonality, serial observation trends, and abrupt demand 
variations [18]. Simultaneously, such causal and multivariate 
models common in economics include promotions, holidays, 
and special events to enhance fidelity in retail forecasts [19] 
[20]; formulations that build upon regressions express these 
exogenous drivers so explicitly [22]. Although they exist, these models have a problem with non-linear interactions and shocks 
that are abrupt characteristic of modern retailing. 
D. Advanced ML Hybrids with Residual Modeling 
Zeng et al., built upon hybridization and added Prophet as a 
residual model into the mix with Bayesian-optimized XGBoost, 
achieving large RMSE/MAPE improvements when residual 
models take advantage of altering loads-an approach that can be 
applied directly to geographically divided retail demand [23]. 
E.  Deep Learning for Complex, High-Volume Data   
Deep learning methods (LSTMs/CNNs/Transformers) have 
surpassed traditional data-mining increasingly as nonlinear, 
multi-modal tasks scale/complexity is increased and have 
performed well in text, image, and speech tasks [24]– [27]. 
Deep models, particularly those used to disaggregate by state 
are not yet fully utilized in retail. 
F. Contemporary hybrid Modeling Approaches 
Recent contributions have extended hybrids further: 
CatBoostXGBoost has categorical processing and boosted 
residual learning, to deal with abrupt demand shocks [28]; a 
TransformerProphetXGBoost combination adds long-term 
procurement and short-term nonlinear effects over space [29]. 
These hybrids achieve good performance, even though this is 
seldom assessed when applied to multi-regional pharmaceutical 
retailing. 
G. Marketing, Competition and Assortment Effects 
The accuracy of the forecasts should be based on driving 
elements of demand: in-store promotions/ displays have been 
shown to boost sales by two or more times [30]; the price and 
cannibalization have the potential to transform product-level 
demand [31]; and companies are increasingly adopting the use 
of competitor promotions/sales to enhance brand forecasts [32]. 
Choice of mix/quantities (assortment optimization) impinges on 
profitability directly, and depends on credible forecasts [33]. 
The existing studies develop few region-sensitive prediction 
models that collaboratively combine both internal (promotions, 
assortment, store format) and external (competitors, holidays) 
factors. 
   The literature continues to progress in reach here, with classical 
statistical baselines giving way to machine learning and deep learning 
hybrids across [12]-[33]. Nevertheless, a number of significant limits 
continue to exist. On the one hand, the work on forecasting at the state 
level in the system of national retail chains remains scarce [12]-[16] 
[17]-[22]. Second, few studies similarly model both internal and 
external drivers in a manner sensitive to their regional variation [23]-
[27], [30]-[33]. Third, despite the potential of hybrid approaches in 
other fields, hybrid approaches have not been sufficiently used in 
pharmaceutical retail forecasting [28], [29]. The work presented here 
fills in such gaps by fitting a Prophet + XGBoost hybrid to daily 
German drugstore chain sales at the state level, with an explicit 
inclusion of promotion, assortment, store format, holidays, and 
neighboring competitor presence, and with a per-state evaluation 
reported against robust baselines. Besides, this study explored the 
Facebook Prophet technique for the first time to forecast retail sales,

III. METHODOLOGY  
A. Data Sources 
The most region-sensitive and correct sales forecasting is a 
strategic prerogative in the context of the intelligently 
developing retail. The proposed research remains a hybrid 
forecasting experiment where Facebook Prophet is combined 
with XGBoost to decompose trends and seasonality changes 
robustly and characterize the remaining nonlinear effects of 
sales regressors. Based on the Rossmann drugstore chain data 
set of Germany, we will predict daily sale of the store at the 
state level, including the effect of internal factors (promotions, 
assortment mix, store format) and external forces (public 
holidays, proximity to competitors). The article is based on the 
dataset of Rossmann Sales Prediction by Kaggle including: 
train.csv: Sales and store level information on a daily basis, 
store.csv: store features of 1,115 branches in Germany.  
All the observations contain daily sales, the store identification, 
and promotion variables, holidays and store characteristics. To 
geographically disaggregate stores it was possible to map 
STORES to the 16 German federal states in the graph using 
location identifiers. That will make state an unambiguous term 
used consistently throughout this paper, as meaning 
Bundesland-level aggregation. Notably, Rossmann dataset does 
not contain product level sales. As compared to previous 
iterations of this piece, we replaced the use of randomly 
imposed product categories to study store-level, and state-level 
daily sales, which fits directly into the contribution of the paper.

B. Data Preparation 
Sundays and holidays: The data has also an Open flag which 
denotes whether a store was in business. We do not train or test 
our models on Sundays or holiday periods, since sales are structurally zero on those trading days (the exception is that we 
have training data available, as Open [the number of trading 
days] can be zero, e. g. you might train on the full year 2019, 
with sales during both Sundays and holidays being zero). In 
case of forecast based on the calendar level, the predicted sales 
are fixed at zero on closed days to carry uniformity. 
Datenfehlern und Outliern: Forward filling was used on 
continuous variables, mode imputation on categorical variables 
to fill in missing values. Winsorized extreme outliers (values 
>3sigma rolling mean) were used to stabilize model training. 
Feature Engineering: The features were broken down into: Int 
(Promotions): Promotions (Promo, Promo2), store type (A-D), 
assort. type (basic, extra, extended). Exogenous influences: 
public holidays (StateHoliday), school holidays, day-of-week 
and proximity of competitors. Regressors were included such 
as lagged sales, rolling means (7/14/30 days) and interaction 
terms (Promo x Holiday). Encoding: Categorical variables (the 
type of store, the type of the assortment, the state) were treated 
as follows: one-hot encoding was applied in categorical 
features, and label-encoding was used in ordinal variables not 
preceded by one-hot encoding. 
This research paper used the characteristics listed in appendix 
A to predict sales in different stores across the states and 
discover what influences those sales. Features presented in 
Appendix A are the ones listed in the Rossmann data with a 
description. These factors have been split into Internal and 
External types. Besides all the other important facts, the data 
also offers details about the kind of store. There are four kinds 
of stores in the organization and they are grouped as A, B, C, 
and D. We assume, the rationale of the classification is 
grounded on the one hand on the area of the store and on the 
other hand on its sales potential. The predicator of ‘Assortment 
type’ has the following choices; (i) Basic, (ii) Extra and (iii) 
Extended and they are coded as ‘a’, ‘b’ or ‘c’. Furthermore, we 
would like to point out that the Rossmann dataset does not hold 
details about any products. Thus, by looking at the external 
sources, we managed to find out which products and brands are 
available in Rossmann stores. These groups of products have 
been randomly arranged in the stores with the RAND function. 
The dataset for the Kaggle competition is mixed with additional 
data on the States and categories a store belongs to using the 
store IDs. After that, any missing entries, extreme values, and 
relationships between variables were managed in the dataset.

for data analysis and we include sales as the output variable.

C. Techniques and Algorithms 
This research will use different data mining methods to try and 
forecast future sales. For the empirical model, the company’s 
promotional strategies, type of merchandise, and store design 
are all identified as internal independent variables, and factors 
such as market competition and public holidays are seen as 
external independent variables. In brief, the methods used are 
as listed below: 
1) Linear Regression: Linear Regression uses the least 
square method to find out the connection between a 
dependent variable and one or more independent 
variables.

2) Decision Tree: This technique works well because it 
is able to deal with both types of variables. Besides, 
the approach is not influenced by the changing size of 
any variable. But, the main flaw in decision trees is 
that they can fit the training data very tightly. Dealing 
with this issue requires you to use strategies such as 
pruning and trimming to manage the size of a tree. 
3) Random Forest: Random Forest was invented to 
resolve some issues that appear in Decision trees. 
Bagging is used to build base models and then the 
group of trees calculates the mean of the results at 
their final nodes. It leaves out the less important 
information and identifies factors that help make the 
prediction more accurate. 
4) XGBoost: Gradient tree boosting is an additional 
ensemble method designed to lower the bias and 
variance in the final model. By following an iterative 
method, the weak individuals are merged into a better 
trained machine. XGBoost applies gradient boosting 
in a powerful way by using only decision trees as 
basic learners and manages their complexity by 
modifying the loss function. 
5) Facebook Prophet: A new way of analyzing time 
series data, known as Facebook Prophet, has also been 
covered in this paper. The library Prophet was created 
by the central data science unit at Facebook. Here, a 
combination model is applied, fitting trends that are 
not straight through yearly, weekly, and daily 
seasonal patterns plus effects of holidays. You get the 
best benefits if the series is clearly shaped by seasonal 
variations and you have data from several seasons. It 
was able to deal with missing data, outliers, and abrupt 
changes in trends. Regarding Facebook’s usage of 
Prophet, Taylor and Letham pointed out that, in many 
situations, its predictions are more reliable and 
accurate than those produced by other methods. The main parts used by Facebook Prophet are trend, 
seasonality, and holidays.

V. DISCUSSION AND RECOMMENDATION  
These findings indicate that the hybrid Prophet and XGBoost 
model outperforms both the classical statistics and the stand-
alone machine learning baselines, with median state-level 
MAPE decreased by 12 to i 8, and estimates both accurate and 
interpretable. The feature importance analysis reveals that 
promotions, assortment type and store format represent the key 
drivers of sales with promotions increasing their weekly sales 
by up to 8 percent in North Rhinewestphalia and only 3 percent 
in Saarland and Extended or Extra assortments consistently 
increasing their sales over Basic assortments by 5-7 percent. 
Regional variation also occurs in store formats, with type B 
stores able to achieve higher sales in the NW and BE and type 
D stores able to achieve higher sales in the TH and HH, and this 
can facilitate the location-specific expansion choices. The 
effects of holidays were modest but significant in the case of 
several states, including BY and SN, and the proper 
consideration of the number of closed days decreased the 
forecasting error by 1520 percent and verified the significance 
of the calendar-conscious planning. According to these 
findings, managers must focus promotions early in the week 
and custom-control intensity by state, expand Extended and 
Extra offerings in high demand states, prioritize the 
introduction of store formats by region, and optimize inventory 
placement to local promotional sensitivity to reduce stock-outs.  
VI. CONCLUSION 
Making accurate and efficient sales forecasts is important in the 
retail industry, as a lot of decisions are made based on them. 
Following this forecasting strategy, the company won’t have to 
pay high costs for storing unsold goods and can earn more 
money. This research examined the performance of various 
machine learning algorithms after considering various aspects 
that affect the process. The use of a recent time series technique 
i.e., Facebook Prophet for forecasting sales is investigated. It 
became clear that XGBoost did better in predicting outcomes 
compared to the other models. In the same way, Facebook 
Prophet provided good results. It is necessary to note that this 
technique solely looks at the sales increase connected to the 
regressor. The results from this project could apply to other 
industries where researchers would try out Facebook Prophet.
